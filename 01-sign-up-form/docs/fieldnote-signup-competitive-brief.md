# Competitive brief: Fieldnote vs Zotero, Notion, Obsidian and Mendeley

Researched Oct 11, 2026 · Companion to the [campaign plan](fieldnote-signup-campaign-plan.md) and the brand voice guidelines

**About the data.** This brief draws on each competitor's website, pricing pages, blogs and changelogs, plus university library guides, forums and review sites. Each claim's source URL is in the research notes behind this brief. Searches turned up **no usable Reddit threads**, so researcher sentiment comes from forums, blogs and library guides, and should be checked by hand before it's quoted in marketing. Items marked _unverified_ come from a single third-party source or are inferred.

---

## 1. Executive summary

Researchers don't lack tools. They split their work across a **reference manager** (Zotero or Mendeley), which stores sources and citations, and a **general note app** (Notion or Obsidian), which holds their thinking. Plugins hold the two together. None of the four keeps highlights, sources and the researcher's own notes together in a notebook for a project or thesis, and none offers true university sign-in.

- **Biggest opportunity:** the gap between the reference library and the note app. Position Fieldnote as the thinking layer that sits on top of Zotero, not as another reference manager. It needs no setup, and it's provided through the researcher's institution. The message: _"Zotero keeps your sources. Fieldnote keeps your thinking."_
- **Biggest threat:** Zotero. It's free, nonprofit, deeply trusted, and now ships a major release every 6–10 weeks. Each release moves further into notes and annotation: note tabs, inserting annotations into documents, and annotating in reading mode. If it adds a strong notes layer, Fieldnote's main gap narrows.

## 4. Positioning map

The axes are **what the tool is for** (managing sources vs holding your thinking) and **how you get it** (set up by the individual vs provided by the institution).

```text
                         Provided by the institution
                                    │
                 Mendeley ●         │         ◆ Fieldnote (target)
        (IP/email check, can lapse)│
                                    │
                 Zotero ●           │
         (email-domain plans)       │
  Manages sources ──────────────────┼────────────────── Holds your thinking
                                    │
                                    │         ● Notion (school-email plan)
                                    │
                                    │         ● Obsidian (DIY, plugins)
                          Set up by the individual
```

The reference managers (Zotero, Mendeley) manage **sources**, and libraries can provide them. The general tools (Notion, Obsidian) hold **thinking**, but each researcher sets them up and wires them to a reference manager alone. **No tool holds research thinking and is also provided by the institution.** That empty top-right quadrant is where Fieldnote should sit.

**Category strategy:** carve out a niche within an existing category. Don't try to beat Zotero as a reference manager. Claim "the notebook for your thesis", and integrate with Zotero instead of replacing it.

## 6. Opportunities

1. **The thinking layer above Zotero.** Every source finds researchers keeping their thinking outside Zotero, and every path there today runs through plugins. Fieldnote can import from Zotero and then keep notes, highlights and sources together, with no setup.
2. **True university sign-in, provided by the institution.** All four competitors check an email address or IP, or offer only individual plans. Signing in with the university login, free for students at participating institutions and covering faculty and staff too, is unclaimed. It also gives librarians stable access that doesn't depend on journal licence negotiations.
3. **Researchers leaving Mendeley.** Libraries are telling users to back up their data and retiring Mendeley tools. Offer an import from Mendeley (through RIS or BibTeX files) and a clear commitment that features won't be retired without warning.
4. **Obsidian burnout.** Pitch to researchers tired of maintaining plugins: "Your thesis, not your setup."
5. **A calm, trustworthy voice.** Notion and Mendeley lead with AI. Fieldnote's understated voice and "every note traces back to its source" set it apart for readers wary of AI-generated citations.
6. **Comparison and migration content.** No competitor writes its own comparison pages, so honest, helpful ones ("Using Fieldnote with Zotero", "Moving notes from Mendeley") are open territory.

## 7. Threats

1. **Zotero's release pace and trust.** It ships every 6–10 weeks, is moving into notes and reading, already sells institution plans, and its nonprofit privacy story is hard to beat. Never attack Zotero, because academics are loyal to it.
2. **Notion's installed base.** Students arrive already using it for free, so switching costs are real.
3. **Obsidian Bases.** Database views over notes are eroding the literature-matrix gap. A first-party Zotero or citation feature from Obsidian would hurt (_speculative_).
4. **Free is the norm.** All four have free tiers. Fieldnote's "free at participating institutions" only works if enough institutions take part, so researchers at other schools need a good email sign-up path.
5. **Unproven claims.** Fieldnote's own claims (institution count, library access, Zotero import) are unconfirmed (see the campaign plan's risks). Competitors back theirs with scale ("100M users", "millions of researchers").

## 8. Recommended actions

**Quick wins (this week)**

1. Adopt the line **"Zotero keeps your sources. Fieldnote keeps your thinking."** for the campaign. Add it to the messaging pillars and to the librarian one-page explainer.
2. Add a **data and export promise** to the one-page explainer: what's exported (Markdown and BibTeX), who can read the notes, and no surprise feature retirements. It answers the main objection from both Zotero and Obsidian users.
3. Swap blog post 2 in the campaign plan to **"Zotero for your sources, Fieldnote for your thinking"**: a no-setup version of the most crowded tutorial topic.

**Strategic moves**

4. **Build the integrations as wedges:** Zotero import first, with two-way annotation sync that never overwrites notes. Mendeley import (RIS or BibTeX files) second, and a Notion export or import third.
5. **Make library channels a competitive weapon.** In the librarian outreach, include an honest comparison sheet covering the access model, privacy and Zotero compatibility. Libraries already compare Mendeley and Zotero for students.
6. **Decide the AI stance on purpose.** Either leave AI out and say so, or build only AI that cites its sources. Don't follow Notion and Mendeley into paywalled AI.
7. **Monitor monthly:** Zotero's blog (release notes), Obsidian's changelog (Bases and citations), Notion's releases (education plan) and Mendeley news from library blogs. Run a manual Reddit pass (r/PhD, r/GradSchool, r/zotero) before quoting any researcher sentiment.
